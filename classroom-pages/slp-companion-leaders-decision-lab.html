1 / 18 Choose where this evidence belongs.

Sales have been declining for more than four years.

Despite repeated leadership efforts, the decline has continued as market conditions, customer behaviour, and competitive pressure have shifted around the business.

Global pricing pressure and currency fluctuations are creating uncertainty about which direction to take.

The business is the only manufacturer in the region, creating an advantage as it can distribute directly from local plants.

The company legacy is built on lasting quality over many years.

Improving local production would require further investment.

The business can strengthen capability and protect quality, but upgrading production capacity will require more capital in the current model.

The business has identified a stronger digital or software-enabled customer experience to improve ordering, service, visibility, and responsiveness.

The business has many brands, plants, and legacy decisions built over more than 100 years.

That history creates strength but it also adds complexity. Not every legacy decision still fits the market the business now has to compete in.

The business has deep manufacturing skills and quality knowledge, but limited importing and global supply chain capability.

The local operating model carries a higher cost base.

High SG&A and skilled labour costs are weighing on the model, while the market is becoming less willing to pay for the full cost of local manufacturing.

The sales team bonus structure is not directly aligned to performance and is not rewarded in real time.

The company has already made a round of layoffs to reduce costs and improve efficiencies.

Cost reduction has helped relieve pressure, but there is still doubt about whether manufacturing everything locally is feasible in this market.

The company has access to a global parent, stronger specifications, and a trusted quality reputation.

This creates an opportunity to source high-quality products through the wider group while still protecting brand standards.

Critical skills are being poached by faster-moving competitors.

Competitors who can move quickly, simplify their model, and compete more aggressively are attracting the skills the business needs for its turnaround.

The business has hired sought-after critical skills to support the turnaround over the last five years.

These skills are expensive, and further recruitment requests are increasing while the business is still under pressure.

Wage negotiations are approaching.

The previous negotiation was difficult, and unions may expect a more favourable outcome this time. But the business is still under financial pressure, which makes labour cost decisions more sensitive and harder to absorb.

The wider economy is putting pressure on demand.

Customers are more price-sensitive, buying decisions are slower, and the market is less forgiving of higher-cost quality products. This market is more favourable for cheaper imports, even if their quality is lower.

Cheaper imported products have continued gaining market share.

Even where local quality remains stronger, customers are increasingly choosing lower-cost imported alternatives because affordability and availability are carrying more weight in the market.

SKU-level analysis showed different cost realities across the portfolio.

Several products cost less to import than to manufacture locally.

Control

Direct decisions, focus, funding, standards, trade-offs.

Influence

People, trust, timing, messaging, stakeholder alignment.

No Control

External forces that must be accepted without becoming excuses.
